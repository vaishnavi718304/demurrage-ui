Trigger Definitions — Plain English

These are the rules the system uses to detect causation. Each is derived directly from the Charter Party terms and the voyage timeline.

T1
Laytime Breach
The total counted hours in port exceeded the allowed laytime granted in the Charter Party. This is the primary indicator of demurrage liability.
T2
Port Congestion
The vessel waited more than 25% of its allowed laytime just to reach a berth. This indicates port congestion contributed to the delay.
T3
Excess Pre-Operations Delay
Significant time passed between berth arrival and the start of cargo operations — exceeding 25% of allowed laytime.
T4
Slow Cargo Operations
The actual cargo loading or discharge operation took longer than the allowed laytime. A direct operational breach.
T7
Add-Hours Dominance
The volume of time added to laytime was at least as large as the full allowed laytime, and the add/deduct ratio was 2 or greater.
T11
High Event Density
The Statement of Facts recorded 8 or more events per day — indicating operational fragmentation or frequent interruptions.
T14
Arrival-to-Berth Delay
More than 50% of the allowed laytime elapsed between vessel arrival and actual berthing — a strong indicator of port-side responsibility.
